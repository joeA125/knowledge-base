# Atom Scott

Researcher at the Graduate School of Informatics, [[nagoya-university]]. Co-author of [[adaptive-action-supervision-multi-agent-rl|Fujii et al. (2023)]].

## Why He Matters Beyond the Co-Authorship

Scott is lead author of **Scott, Fujii & Onishi (2022)**, *How does AI play football? An analysis of RL and real-world football strategies* (ICAART), which the vault has flagged as **cited, not held** across three ingests — on [[reinforcement-learning]], [[google-research-football]] and [[multi-agent-reinforcement-learning]] — because it is the one cited work bearing directly on the **simulator transfer question**.

That paper compares ball-passing behaviour between [[google-research-football|GFootball]] agents and professional players. It is, on the vault's reading, the closest thing the field has to a direct measurement of the gap that [[domain-adaptation|Real-to-Sim]] adaptation exists to bridge.

**It is now the highest-value acquisition target arising from this ingest**, replacing arXiv:2305.13030 — which was the previous target, has been acquired, and did not settle what it was expected to settle.

## Position in the Group

Fujii et al. (2023) describes the state of play Scott's own work established:

> the ball-passing behaviors in artificial agents of Google Research Football and professional football players were compared (Scott et al., 2022), but a gap still exists between these forward and backward approaches

So the sequence within the group is legible: **Scott measures the gap; Fujii et al. propose a method to bridge it; the method does not close it in football.** Scott appears on both papers.

His presence on the author list of the paper that builds [[nfootball]] — after having worked with GFootball — is suggestive of where the decision to abandon GFootball came from, though nothing in the source states this and it should be read as inference from co-authorship only.

# Yoshinobu Kawahara

Researcher at the Graduate School of Information Science and Technology, [[osaka-university]], with an affiliation at the RIKEN Center for Advanced Intelligence Project. Co-author of [[adaptive-action-supervision-multi-agent-rl|Fujii et al. (2023)]].

## The Methodological Collaboration

Kawahara appears with [[keisuke-fujii]] and [[naoya-takeishi]] on the same recurring cluster of **cited, not held** works that sit outside the applied football line — multi-person policy learning under partial observation (arXiv:2007.03155), interaction rules from multi-animal trajectories (NeurIPS 2021), and counterfactual treatment outcomes in multi-agent scenarios (arXiv:2206.01900).

He and Takeishi are absent from every applied football-metric paper the vault holds ([[vdep]], [[gvdep]], [[c-obso]], [[drso]], [[nmstpp]], [[xsot]]) and present on the one that treats football as a **test case for a general multi-agent method**. See [[naoya-takeishi]], where the pattern is set out, and [[domain-adaptation]].

**This is the clearest instance in the vault of a research group operating in two registers** — an applied sports-metric line and a methodological multi-agent line — with a partly disjoint author set and a single senior author bridging them. It has a practical consequence: the methodological papers state their football results as *demonstrations*, and should not be read as claims about football the way [[c-obso|C-OBSO]] or [[vdep|VDEP]] can be.

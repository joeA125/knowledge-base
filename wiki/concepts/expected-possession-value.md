# Expected Possession Value (EPV)

EPV ([[multiresolution-stochastic-process-nba-possessions|Cervone et al., 2016]]) is the expected number of points a basketball possession will yield, conditional on everything observed up to the current instant:

$$\nu_t = \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{F}_t^{(Z)}]$$

where $X \in \{0, 2, 3\}$ is the possession's eventual point total and $\mathcal{F}_t^{(Z)}$ is the filtration generated by [[optical-tracking-data]] over $[0, t]$.

## The Stock Ticker Analogy

EPV updates continuously as players move, much as a stock price updates on new information. In the paper's worked example, a Miami Heat possession opens at EPV ≈ 1.0, rises to 1.34 as Norris Cole drives the basket, collapses to 0.77 as he dribbles past it into pressure, resets to 1.00 on a pass, and climbs to 1.62 as LeBron James splits the defence for a layup. Every inflection corresponds to something a coach would recognise.

## Why It Must Be a Martingale

EPV is a conditional expectation, so by the tower property it is a [[martingale]]: $\mathbb{E}[\nu_{t+\epsilon} \mid \mathcal{F}_t] = \nu_t$. This is not incidental — it is what licenses the stock-ticker reading. Changes in the curve then reflect what players actually did, rather than artifacts of the estimator.

This rules out estimating EPV by regressing game-state features directly onto outcomes. Such an approach would produce a number in $[0,3]$ but with no guarantee of stochastic consistency, so its fluctuations would be uninterpretable. Instead EPV must derive from a genuine [[multiresolution-modelling|stochastic process model]] of how possessions evolve.

## Estimation

EPV is computed by [[multiresolution-modelling|multiresolution modelling]]: a coarsened [[markov-game|semi-Markov chain]] over discrete states supplies $\mathbb{E}[X \mid C_{\delta_t}]$ cheaply, while continuous models of player movement and event hazards supply $\mathbb{P}(C_{\delta_t} \mid \mathcal{F}_t^{(Z)})$ — requiring forecasts only until the next pass, shot, or turnover.

## Derived Metrics

### EPV-Added (EPVA)
Because EPV is a martingale, $\mathbb{E}[\nu_{t+\epsilon} - \nu_t] = 0$, so a player's raw EPV changes average to zero and cannot be aggregated directly. EPVA sidesteps this by measuring value **relative to a hypothetical league-average player** placed in identical situations, summed over all touches and normalised per game. 2013–14 leaders: Durant (3.26), James (2.96), Calderon (2.79).

Averaging per game rather than per touch deliberately rewards usage — otherwise one-dimensional specialists who only touch the ball in favourable spots would dominate.

### Shot Satisfaction
For each shot, compares EPV at the attempt against the expected value of the player's available passes. Positive values mean shooting was the right call. Highest for three-point specialists and at-rim finishers; lowest for mid-range shooters.

## Relation to Soccer's VAEP

EPV and [[vaep]] answer the same question in different sports and data regimes:

| | EPV (basketball) | [[vaep]] (soccer) |
|---|---|---|
| Data | [[optical-tracking-data]], 25 Hz | [[event-stream-data]], discrete actions |
| Model | Bayesian [[stochastic-process]] | [[gradient-boosting]] classifiers |
| Output | Continuous curve $\nu_t$ | Per-action value $V(a_i)$ |
| Structure | [[martingale]] guaranteed | Not guaranteed |
| Cost | Very high (461 processors) | Modest |
| Portability | Requires camera installations | Widely available data |

[[evaluating-football-player-actions|Decroos et al. (2019)]] cite this work as related. Both descend from the same lineage of modelling sport as a [[markov-game]].

## Limitations

- Excludes fouls entirely.
- On-ball offence only — defensive value and off-ball movement are invisible.
- The league-average baseline used in EPVA is a hypothetical construct and a heavy extrapolation.

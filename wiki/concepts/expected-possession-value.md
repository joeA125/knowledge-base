# Expected Possession Value (EPV)

EPV ([[multiresolution-stochastic-process-nba-possessions|Cervone et al., 2016]]) is the expected number of points a basketball possession will yield, conditional on everything observed up to the current instant:

$$\nu_t = \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{F}_t^{(Z)}]$$

where $X \in \{0, 2, 3\}$ is the possession's eventual point total and $\mathcal{F}_t^{(Z)}$ is the filtration generated by [[optical-tracking-data]] over $[0, t]$.

## ⚠️ Terminology Collision

"Expected possession value" denotes two different things in the sports analytics literature:

- **Basketball (this page):** Cervone et al.'s specific continuous-time [[martingale]] construction estimated from tracking data.
- **Soccer:** a *category label* for possession-based Markov models, used this way by [[on-ball-actions-football-xt-vs-vaep|Van Roy et al. (2020)]]. The category includes [[expected-threat|xT]], Rudd (2011), Mackay (2017), and Yam (2019) — all far simpler than the basketball model.

The soccer usage is broader and the two should not be conflated. Fernández, Bornn & Cervone (2019) further muddy this by bringing a genuine Cervone-style EPV framework *to* soccer using tracking data.

## The Stock Ticker Analogy

EPV updates continuously as players move, much as a stock price updates on new information. In the paper's worked example, a Miami Heat possession opens at EPV ≈ 1.0, rises to 1.34 as Norris Cole drives the basket, collapses to 0.77 as he dribbles past it into pressure, resets to 1.00 on a pass, and climbs to 1.62 as LeBron James splits the defence for a layup.

## Why It Must Be a Martingale

EPV is a conditional expectation, so by the tower property it is a [[martingale]]: $\mathbb{E}[\nu_{t+\epsilon} \mid \mathcal{F}_t] = \nu_t$. This is what licenses the stock-ticker reading — changes in the curve reflect what players actually did, rather than artifacts of the estimator.

This rules out estimating EPV by regressing game-state features directly onto outcomes. Such an approach would produce a number in $[0,3]$ but with no guarantee of stochastic consistency. Instead EPV must derive from a genuine [[multiresolution-modelling|stochastic process model]].

## Estimation

Computed by [[multiresolution-modelling|multiresolution modelling]]: a coarsened [[markov-game|semi-Markov chain]] supplies $\mathbb{E}[X \mid C_{\delta_t}]$ cheaply, while continuous models of player movement and event hazards supply $\mathbb{P}(C_{\delta_t} \mid \mathcal{F}_t^{(Z)})$ — requiring forecasts only until the next pass, shot, or turnover.

## Derived Metrics

### EPV-Added (EPVA)
Because EPV is a martingale, $\mathbb{E}[\nu_{t+\epsilon} - \nu_t] = 0$, so a player's raw EPV changes average to zero and cannot be aggregated directly. EPVA measures value **relative to a hypothetical league-average player** in identical situations, normalised per game. 2013–14 leaders: Durant (3.26), James (2.96), Calderon (2.79).

### Shot Satisfaction
Compares EPV at a shot attempt against the expected value of available passes. Highest for three-point specialists and at-rim finishers; lowest for mid-range shooters.

## Position Among Action Valuation Frameworks

EPV sits at the far end of the [[action-valuation]] design space — the richest state representation, the strongest theoretical guarantees, and by far the highest cost:

| | [[expected-threat\|xT]] | [[vaep]] | EPV |
|---|---|---|---|
| State | Ball's zone | Last 3 actions | Full tracking history |
| Data | [[event-stream-data\|Event stream]] | Event stream | [[optical-tracking-data\|Tracking]] |
| Martingale | No | No | **Yes** |
| Cost | Trivial | Modest | 461 processors |

The authors acknowledge the computational requirements "likely limit EPV discussions to academic circles and professional basketball teams with access to the appropriate resources."

## Limitations

- Excludes fouls entirely.
- On-ball offence only — defensive value and off-ball movement are largely invisible.
- The league-average baseline used in EPVA is a hypothetical construct and a heavy extrapolation.

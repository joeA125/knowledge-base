# Martingale EPV (Cervone et al.)

The specific basketball [[expected-possession-value]] construction of [[multiresolution-stochastic-process-nba-possessions|Cervone, D'Amour, Bornn & Goldsberry (2016)]]: the expected number of points a possession will yield, conditional on everything observed up to the current instant, estimated in continuous time from [[optical-tracking-data]].

$$\nu_t = \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{F}_t^{(Z)}]$$

where $X \in \{0, 2, 3\}$ is the possession's eventual point total and $\mathcal{F}_t^{(Z)}$ is the filtration generated by tracking data over $[0, t]$.

> **Naming.** This page covers one specific model. "Expected possession value" is also used as a broad *category label* in soccer analytics, and as the name of a distinct soccer framework by overlapping authors. See [[expected-possession-value]] for the umbrella concept and the full disambiguation. "Martingale EPV" is used here because the [[martingale]] property is precisely what separates this construction from everything else sharing its name.

## The Stock Ticker Analogy

EPV updates continuously as players move, much as a stock price updates on new information. In the paper's worked example, a Miami Heat possession opens at EPV ≈ 1.0, rises to 1.34 as Norris Cole drives the basket, collapses to 0.77 as he dribbles past it into pressure, resets to 1.00 on a pass, and climbs to 1.62 as LeBron James splits the defence for a layup. Every inflection corresponds to something a coach would recognise.

## Why It Must Be a Martingale

EPV is a conditional expectation, so by the tower property it is a [[martingale]]: $\mathbb{E}[\nu_{t+\epsilon} \mid \mathcal{F}_t] = \nu_t$. This is not incidental — it is what licenses the stock-ticker reading. Changes in the curve then reflect what players actually did, rather than artifacts of the estimator. The authors call this *stochastic consistency*.

This **rules out estimating EPV by regressing game-state features directly onto outcomes.** Such an approach would produce a number in $[0,3]$ but with no guarantee of consistency, so its fluctuations would be uninterpretable. Instead the estimate must derive from a genuine stochastic process model of how possessions evolve.

**No other framework in this vault has this property.** [[expected-threat|xT]], [[vaep]], [[pass-carry-reward|PCR]], and the soccer possession-based models all produce values without a martingale guarantee.

## Estimation

Computed by [[multiresolution-modelling|multiresolution modelling]]. Under a semi-Markov assumption and a decoupling assumption:

$$\nu_t = \sum_{c \in \mathcal{C}} \mathbb{E}[X \mid C_{\delta_t} = c] \; \mathbb{P}(C_{\delta_t} = c \mid \mathcal{F}_t^{(Z)})$$

A coarsened semi-[[markov-game|Markov chain]] supplies the first factor by transition-matrix algebra; continuous models of player movement and [[competing-risks|event hazards]] supply the second, requiring forecasts only until the next pass, shot, or turnover.

Supporting machinery: [[gaussian-process]] spatial effects in a functional basis, [[car-prior|CAR priors]] sharing information across similar players, [[non-negative-matrix-factorization|NMF]] for both the player-similarity structure and the spatial bases, and [[inla]] for inference at scale.

## The Soccer Descendant, and What It Gave Up

[[expected-value-possession-framework|Fernández, Bornn & Cervone (2020)]] carry this programme to soccer, with [[luke-bornn|Bornn]] and [[daniel-cervone|Cervone]] on both papers. The conceptual object is the same — a continuously-updating estimate of a possession's worth from tracking data — but almost every design decision is reversed:

| | Martingale EPV (2016) | Soccer EPV (2020) |
|---|---|---|
| Sport | Basketball | Soccer |
| Target | Points $X \in \{0,2,3\}$ | $G \in \{-1, 1\}$, which team scores next |
| Estimation | One Bayesian process model | **Nine supervised components, [[structured-model-decomposition\|decomposed]]** |
| Martingale guarantee | **Yes** | No |
| Interpretability route | Stochastic consistency | Decomposition along coach-legible axes |
| Cost | 461 processors | Real-time (899+ examples/sec) |

The reversal on estimation is worth dwelling on. The 2016 paper argues explicitly that supervised regression of features onto outcomes cannot deliver an interpretable value curve, because nothing constrains its fluctuations. The 2020 framework is, structurally, supervised regression of features onto outcomes — and Cervone is an author of both.

This is not a contradiction so much as a **change in what interpretability is taken to mean**. In 2016 it comes from a mathematical property: the curve is trustworthy because the estimator is stochastically consistent. In 2020 it comes from structure: the estimate is trustworthy because it decomposes into pieces a coach can inspect and query, each independently [[probability-calibration|calibrated]].

Both are defensible. They are not equivalent, and the trade is real — the soccer framework buys tractability, real-time inference and off-ball coverage, and pays with the guarantee that made the basketball curve readable as a ticker. Whether the soccer EPV curve's wiggles are signal or estimator noise is, strictly, unestablished.

That the same authors made opposite choices five years apart is the clearest evidence in this vault that the martingale property is a **cost** as well as a virtue: it is what forced 461 processors, and abandoning it is what made a usable coaching tool possible.

## Derived Metrics

### EPV-Added (EPVA)
Because EPV is a martingale, $\mathbb{E}[\nu_{t+\epsilon} - \nu_t] = 0$ — a player's raw EPV changes average to zero and cannot be aggregated directly. EPVA measures value **relative to a hypothetical league-average player** placed in identical situations, summed over touches and normalised per game.

2013–14 leaders: Durant (3.26), James (2.96), Calderon (2.79), Nowitzki (2.69), Curry (2.50).

Averaging per game rather than per touch deliberately rewards usage — otherwise specialists who only touch the ball in favourable spots would dominate.

Note that the soccer framework produces **no analogous player metric at all**, and this is why: without the martingale property there is no zero-mean baseline forcing the issue, but also no principled aggregation. It values situations, not seasons.

### Shot Satisfaction
For each shot, compares EPV at the attempt against the expected value of available passes. Highest for three-point specialists and at-rim finishers (Plumlee 0.35, Prigioni 0.31); lowest for mid-range shooters (Aldridge, Garnett).

This is an early version of the realised-versus-available comparison that [[probability-surface|pass surfaces]] later generalise across the whole pitch — here restricted to the passes actually available to teammates rather than every location.

## Cost

The defining practical fact about this model: microtransition fitting runs across **461 processors** at up to 18 hours each; each macrotransition type takes ~24 hours on 120GB RAM. The authors concede this "likely limits EPV discussions to academic circles and professional basketball teams with access to the appropriate resources."

## Limitations

- Excludes fouls entirely (the data does not specify foul type).
- On-ball offence only — defensive value and off-ball movement largely invisible.
- Rebounds use constant team-level probabilities rather than conditioning on rebounder positions.
- The league-average baseline in EPVA is hypothetical and a heavy extrapolation.
- The semi-Markov assumption is violated by scripted set plays.

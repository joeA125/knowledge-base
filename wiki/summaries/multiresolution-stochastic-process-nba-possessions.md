# A Multiresolution Stochastic Process Model for Predicting Basketball Possession Outcomes

**Authors:** [[daniel-cervone]], [[alex-damour]], [[luke-bornn]], [[kirk-goldsberry]]
**Affiliations:** New York University, Harvard University, Simon Fraser University
**Published:** 2016 (Journal of the American Statistical Association)

## Key Contribution

Introduces [[martingale-epv|expected possession value (EPV)]] for basketball — the expected number of points a possession will yield, conditional on everything observed so far. EPV is estimated in real time from NBA [[optical-tracking-data]] via a [[multiresolution-modelling|multiresolution]] stochastic process model that combines a fully continuous model of player movement with a coarsened [[markov-game|Markov chain]] over discrete game states.

> Note on naming: "expected possession value" is also used in soccer as a *category label* for a family of much simpler models. This page and [[martingale-epv]] cover the specific basketball construction; [[expected-possession-value]] covers the shared idea.

## The Data

In 2013 the NBA installed optical tracking in all 30 arenas, capturing 2D positions of all 10 players and 3D ball position at **25 Hz** — over 1 billion space-time observations per season. This is a step change from the "box score" summaries (points, rebounds, assists) that earlier metrics like Player Efficiency Rating and Adjusted Plus/Minus rely on.

## Defining EPV

$$\nu_t = \mathbb{E}[X \mid \mathcal{F}_t^{(Z)}]$$

where $X \in \{0, 2, 3\}$ is the possession's point total and $\mathcal{F}_t^{(Z)}$ is the filtration generated by tracking data up to time $t$. EPV behaves "like a stock ticker" — an instantaneous valuation updated continuously as the possession unfolds.

## Two Estimator Criteria

1. **Stochastic consistency.** EPV must be a genuine conditional expectation so that changes in the curve reflect players' actions, not analysis artifacts. This rules out marginal regression approaches, which break the [[martingale]] structure.
2. **Sensitivity without intractability.** A pure Markov chain approach requires discretising the tracking data, discarding useful spatial information.

The [[multiresolution-modelling|multiresolution]] approach satisfies both by modelling at two resolutions simultaneously.

## The Coarsened Process

States $\mathcal{C} = \mathcal{C}_{poss} \cup \mathcal{C}_{trans} \cup \mathcal{C}_{end}$:
- **$\mathcal{C}_{poss}$**: (ballcarrier ID) × (one of 7 court regions) × (defended within 5 feet?)
- **$\mathcal{C}_{trans}$**: shot in progress, pass in flight, turnover in progress, rebound in progress
- **$\mathcal{C}_{end}$**: made 2pt, made 3pt, end of possession (values 2, 3, 0)

## Key Theorem

Under the semi-Markov assumption (A1) and the decoupling assumption (A2) — that passing through a transition state renders prior history uninformative:

$$\nu_t = \sum_{c \in \mathcal{C}} \mathbb{E}[X \mid C_{\delta_t} = c] \; \mathbb{P}(C_{\delta_t} = c \mid \mathcal{F}_t^{(Z)})$$

The first factor is cheap (Markov chain algebra); the second requires forecasting full-resolution data only until the *next* macrotransition, not to the end of the possession.

## Four Transition Models

- **(M1) Microtransition** — infinitesimal player movement given no ball movement. Offence via a Taylor expansion with spatially varying acceleration fields; defence anchored to a linear combination of basket, ball, and guarded offender (following Franks et al. 2015).
- **(M2) Macrotransition entry** — [[competing-risks]] hazards for 6 event types (4 pass options, shot, turnover), log-linear in situational covariates plus a [[gaussian-process]] spatial effect.
- **(M3) Macrotransition exit** — outcome of the event; for shots, a logistic shot-probability model sharing the same parameterisation.
- **(M4) Transition matrix** — for the coarsened chain, with counts Rao-Blackwellised by substituting expected counts derived from the fitted hazards.

## Inference

- Spatial effects given a low-dimensional [[gaussian-process]] representation via functional bases (383-vertex triangular mesh, GMRF/Matérn approximation), reduced to $d = 10$ bases by [[non-negative-matrix-factorization|NMF]].
- Player-specific coefficients share information through a [[car-prior|conditional autoregressive prior]] over an adjacency matrix of "similar" players — similarity defined by NMF on court-occupancy distributions, not by listed position (LeBron James and Steve Novak are both small forwards but behave nothing alike).
- Fitted via [[inla|integrated nested Laplace approximation]]. Microtransition models run across 461 processors, ≤18 hours each; each macrotransition type takes ~24 hours on 120GB RAM.

## Results

Out-of-sample log-likelihood improves monotonically as the model adds situational covariates, then spatial effects, then full hierarchical shrinkage — justifying the CAR prior structure.

### EPVA (EPV-Added)

Because EPV is a [[martingale]], $\mathbb{E}[\nu_{t+\epsilon} - \nu_t] = 0$, so raw EPV changes cannot be aggregated per player. EPVA instead measures value relative to a hypothetical league-average player receiving the ball in identical circumstances:

$$\text{EPVA}(\ell) = \frac{1}{\#\text{games}} \sum_{\{t_s, t_e\}} \nu_{t_e} - \nu^{r(\ell)}_{t_s}$$

2013–14 top 5: Kevin Durant (3.26), LeBron James (2.96), Jose Calderon (2.79), Dirk Nowitzki (2.69), Stephen Curry (2.50). Bottom: Rudy Gay (−2.59), Ricky Rubio (−2.36), Rajon Rondo (−2.27).

### Shot Satisfaction

Compares EPV at a shot attempt against the expected value of the player's available passing options. Highest: Mason Plumlee (0.35), Pablo Prigioni (0.31), Mike Miller (0.27) — three-point specialists and at-rim finishers. Lowest: mid-range shooters (Aldridge, Garnett) and weak shooters (Rubio). Values are mostly positive league-wide, so most players do help their team by shooting rather than passing.

## Limitations

- The semi-Markov assumption is violated by pre-set plays that string together scripted sequences.
- Fouls are excluded entirely (the data does not specify foul type).
- Rebounds use constant team-level probabilities rather than conditioning on rebounder positions.
- The league-average baseline in EPVA is a heavy extrapolation — a hypothetical player is assumed to face every situation an actual player faced.
- EPVA covers only on-ball offence; defence and off-ball movement are out of scope.

## Relation to Other Vault Sources

EPV is the basketball analogue of soccer's [[vaep]] — [[evaluating-football-player-actions|Decroos et al. (2019)]] cite this paper as related work. The key contrast: VAEP operates on discrete [[event-stream-data]] with [[gradient-boosting]], while EPV operates on continuous [[optical-tracking-data]] with a Bayesian [[stochastic-process|stochastic process]] model. VAEP is cheaper and more portable; EPV is finer-grained and preserves [[martingale]] structure.
